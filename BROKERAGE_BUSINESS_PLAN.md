## 5 · Industry & Market Analysis

### 5.1 · National brokerage landscape

- **TAM (US property brokerage):** ~$95B 2025 · ~$115B by 2028 (Armstrong & Associates).
- **Fragmentation:** Top 25 brokerages control ~45% of TAM. The long tail — single-agent and sub-$10M shops — collectively brokers more freight than the top 5 combined.
- **Margin band:** Industry settled gross margin averages **14–16%**. Operator-built brokerages with disciplined carrier vetting and lane discipline run **18–24%**.
- **Tailwinds:** Shipper appetite for asset-light fulfillment; structural retirement of internal traffic teams; freight-tech maturity allowing 2-person shops to operate like 10-person ones.
- **Headwinds:** Cyclical capacity gluts (2023–2024 trough); fraud & double-brokering surge; FMCSA enforcement tightening (BMC-84 enforcement, BOC-3 reverification).

### 5.2 · Minnesota & Twin Cities micro-market

Minnesota generates **~125M outbound freight tons annually** and consumes a near-equivalent inbound volume. The Twin Cities metro is the **largest inland distribution hub between Chicago and Seattle** and the natural broker entry point.

**Industry composition that feeds Orisei's pipeline:**

| Sector | Representative MN shippers | Freight character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food & beverage | General Mills · Schwan's · Hormel · Cargill | Reefer + dry van · daily outbound DCs |
| Big-box retail DC | Target HQ DCs · Best Buy · Polaris parts | High-volume TL · Midwest hub-and-spoke |
| Industrial / OEM | 3M · Tennant · Polaris · Toro · Donaldson | Mixed mode · international + domestic |
| Medical devices | Medtronic · Boston Scientific · Smiths Medical | Expedited + air interline |
| Agriculture | CHS · Land O'Lakes · Cargill | Hopper + flatbed + intermodal |
| Fabricated metals | Pentair · Graco | Flatbed + step-deck |

**Lane density advantages.**

- **MSP → Chicago (~410 mi):** Daily volume; reliable rpm; ideal training lane for a new brokerage.
- **MSP → Dallas (~950 mi):** Strong reefer / dry van triangulation.
- **MSP → Pacific NW (Portland/Seattle, ~1,650 mi):** Higher rpm reefer lane; CHS / Cargill outbound.
- **MSP → Northeast (Newark, ~1,250 mi):** High-rate medical & retail.
- **MSP ⇆ Duluth / Iron Range:** Flatbed / bulk lanes; lower competition.

### 5.3 · Customer profile (Orisei ICP)

**Primary ICP:** Minnesota-headquartered industrial shipper, **$50M–$500M revenue**, 3–25 loads/week, traffic team of 1–3 (or none — outsourced to incumbent broker who is mediocre).

**Secondary ICP:** Out-of-state shippers needing **inbound coverage into MN/WI/IA** — Orisei's local advantage.

**Buyer:** Logistics Manager, Supply Chain Director, or CFO at sub-$200M shops.

### 8.4 · Freight Sourcing Mix — boards are the garnish, the shipper book is the engine

**The board reality (rev-3).** Load boards are, for a broker, a **carrier-sourcing and market-intelligence tool
— not a freight source**. Roughly 90%+ of postings on DAT/Truckstop were posted by other brokers; moving those
loads without written consent is **double-brokering** — illegal, banned by every board, and the #1 fraud pattern
under FMCSA enforcement. Shipper-direct board postings exist but are a small, hyper-contested slice at 3–8%
margins with zero relationship stickiness. Market data (FreightWaves): only ~14% of loads move via boards;
~63% come from direct shipper relationships.

**Orisei's sourcing mix by phase:**

| Phase | Shipper-direct /day | Board & spot supplemental /day | Total /day |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Mo 1–3 (launch) | 1–3 | 1–2 | **2–5** |
| Mo 4–9 (ramp) | 4–8 | 1–2 | **5–10** |
| Mo 10+ (scale) | 10–16 | 2–4 | **12–20** |

**What the boards ARE for:** (1) posting our shipper freight to source carriers in minutes; (2) DAT RateView
market pricing to quote credibly from day 1; (3) **prospecting intelligence** — seeing which shippers post
direct, then calling them to go direct; (4) legitimate backhaul/co-brokered supplemental volume via the
Backhaul Hunter, always with written co-broker agreements.

**Compliance guardrail:** Orisei never re-brokers another broker's posted load. Every load on our book has a
direct broker-shipper agreement or a written co-brokerage agreement. No exceptions — the MC's reputation is the
company's single most valuable asset.

### 15A.3 The funding ladder

2. **Quick-pay as a profit center.** Charge carriers 2% for 2-day pay (sandbox already books quick-pay income).

3. **Year 1 — retain what the ramp allows.** Distributions are capped at $30K combined in Year 1; the plan

5. **Guardrails.** Credit-check every new shipper (board credit data); cap any one shipper at 20% of AR; bad-debt

### 15A.4 DSO Management Playbook — will factoring carry the weight?

**Short answer: yes for cash, through Month 9 — but at a price, and with gaps.** Non-recourse factoring at a
92% advance cuts the full-volume cash float from ≈ **$513K** (self-funded, 18-day DSO–DPO gap on $10.4M) to
≈ **$85–130K** (8% holdbacks + fees + 1–2 day advance lag) — comfortably inside partner capital plus retained
earnings. What factoring does **not** solve:

| Gap | Exposure |
| --- | --- |
| 8% holdback reserve stays illiquid until the shipper pays | ≈ $84K locked at full volume |
| Fees compound at volume | 3.25% × $10.4M ≈ **$210K/yr** — 22% of gross margin — vs $60–80K on a bank AR line |
| Disputed / short-paid invoices charged back (fraud & dispute carve-outs survive "non-recourse") | Case-by-case |
| Enterprise shippers that prohibit invoice assignment | Those loads self-fund |
| Factor concentration limits (~25% per shipper) can freeze advances | Volume-dependent |

So the plan treats factoring as the **backstop**, not the strategy — the strategy is to shrink DSO itself:

**Lever 1 · Prevent (before the load).** Credit-check every new shipper via board credit data (floor score 87);
written net-30 terms + late-fee clause in every broker-shipper agreement; credit limit = 2 weeks of volume until
3 clean payment cycles; cap any one shipper at 20% of AR.

**Lever 2 · Accelerate (day 0–5).** Same-day invoicing the moment POD lands; first-pass-complete doc packets
(POD + rate con + lumper receipts — missing docs cause ~40% of slow-pays); EDI/e-invoicing where supported
(saves 3–7 days); selective 1%/10-net-30 early-pay discount for the two largest accounts (cheaper than factoring
the same dollars).

**Lever 3 · Collect (day 25–50).** Automated dunning cadence: day-25 reminder → day-32 call → day-40 escalation
